How to Land Your First Web Development Job: Tips for Freshers

November 22, 2025 7 min read Codeksha Team

Getting your first developer job is often harder than learning to code, mostly because it depends on things tutorials don't teach - how you present your skills and how you talk about your work.

Build a Portfolio Before You Apply Anywhere

A portfolio with 2-3 complete, working projects (not tutorial clones) is worth more to most employers than a certificate alone. Include live, deployed links and clean, readable code on GitHub - employers do check.

Understand the Projects You've Built, Deeply

Interviewers will ask why you made specific decisions in your projects. Being able to explain your reasoning - why you chose a certain approach, what you'd do differently now - matters more than having built something perfect.

Don't Skip the Basics in Interview Prep

Even for junior roles, expect questions on fundamentals: how the DOM works, basic data structures, how HTTP requests work, or simple debugging scenarios. These come up more often than obscure framework trivia.

Apply Broadly, Including Smaller Companies and Agencies

Smaller companies and agencies often have more flexibility to hire and train freshers than large companies with rigid requirements. They can also give you broader, faster real-world experience.

Use Your Network, Even a Small One

A referral from an instructor, classmate, or anyone in the industry dramatically increases the chance your application gets seen. This is one real advantage of classroom-based training over solo online learning - you build a network of instructors and peers already in the field.

Be Honest About What You Don't Know Yet

Overstating your skills in an interview usually backfires quickly once you're asked to demonstrate them. Being honest about your current level while showing genuine eagerness to learn is generally received far better.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a computer science degree to get a web development job?

No - many working developers come from non-CS backgrounds. Demonstrated skill, a solid portfolio, and practical project experience typically matter more to employers than the degree itself.

How many projects should be in my portfolio?

Quality over quantity - 2 to 4 well-built, complete projects that you can explain in depth are generally more effective than a long list of shallow ones.
